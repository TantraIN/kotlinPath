<KeyIdea>
Every view needs at least one **horizontal** and one **vertical** constraint, or it lands at the
top-left corner. Chains, guidelines and barriers exist so that relationships between views never
require another level of nesting.
</KeyIdea>

## Why this matters

The previous lesson ended on the cost of nesting: every level is another measure pass, and a
weighted level is two. A screen built from four nested `LinearLayout`s pays that on every frame.

`ConstraintLayout` says the same thing in one level. The trade is that positions become
relationships you declare rather than a structure you build.

## A constraint is a pair of sides

```xml title="res/layout/card.xml" numbered
<androidx.constraintlayout.widget.ConstraintLayout
    android:layout_width="match_parent"
    android:layout_height="wrap_content">

    <ImageView
        android:id="@+id/avatar"
        android:layout_width="48dp"
        android:layout_height="48dp"
        app:layout_constraintStart_toStartOf="parent"
        app:layout_constraintTop_toTopOf="parent" />

    <TextView
        android:id="@+id/title"
        android:layout_width="0dp"
        android:layout_height="wrap_content"
        app:layout_constraintStart_toEndOf="@id/avatar"
        app:layout_constraintEnd_toEndOf="parent"
        app:layout_constraintTop_toTopOf="@id/avatar" />
</androidx.constraintlayout.widget.ConstraintLayout>
```

Read each attribute as *my side, to your side*: `Start_toEndOf` means "my start edge attaches to
your end edge". Constrain both opposite sides and the view centres between them.

## Chains distribute a row

A chain is two or more views constrained to each other in both directions. The first view in the
chain — the **head** — carries the style.

| `layout_constraintHorizontal_chainStyle` | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `spread` | Space divided evenly around every view (the default) |
| `spread_inside` | Outer views pinned to the edges, space between the rest |
| `packed` | Views pushed together, the group positioned by `bias` |

Give the chain members `0dp` and a `layout_constraintHorizontal_weight` and you get exactly what
`layout_weight` did in a `LinearLayout` — without the extra measure pass.

## Guidelines and barriers are not the same thing

<Compare
  left={{
    title: "Guideline",
    subtitle: "A fixed invisible line",
    accent: "sky",
    points: [
      "+ Placed at a percent or a fixed dp from an edge",
      "+ Right for a design grid — a 16dp margin column",
      "+ Costs nothing; it is never drawn",
      "- Does not move when content changes",
    ],
  }}
  right={{
    title: "Barrier",
    subtitle: "A line that follows content",
    accent: "emerald",
    points: [
      "+ Sits at the edge of whichever referenced view is largest",
      "+ Right for a label column where the longest label wins",
      "+ Survives translation, where label lengths change",
      "- References views by id, so it needs maintaining",
    ],
  }}
/>

```xml title="Barrier.xml" numbered
<androidx.constraintlayout.widget.Barrier
    android:id="@+id/labelEnd"
    android:layout_width="wrap_content"
    android:layout_height="wrap_content"
    app:barrierDirection="end"
    app:constraint_referenced_ids="nameLabel,emailLabel,phoneLabel" />
```

Every value field then constrains its start to `@id/labelEnd`, and the column lines up no matter
which label is longest — including after a translator makes one of them twice as long.

## Under the hood: it is a solver

<Timeline>
  <TimelineItem title="Constraints become equations" meta="not instructions">
    The layout is a system of linear relationships, solved by a Cassowary-style solver. You are
    describing a result, not a sequence of steps.
  </TimelineItem>
  <TimelineItem title="Which is why order does not matter" meta="mostly">
    Declaring a view before the view it references is fine. The solver sees the whole system at
    once.
  </TimelineItem>
  <TimelineItem title="And why circles are an error" meta="not a crash">
    Two views constrained to each other's edges with no fixed point have no solution. The editor
    warns, and at run time one of them collapses.
  </TimelineItem>
  <TimelineItem title="One flat pass" meta="the payoff">
    Because the whole thing is solved together, the tree is measured once rather than once per
    nesting level.
  </TimelineItem>
</Timeline>

<Detail summary="`bias`, and what it does when both sides are constrained">
Constrain both opposite sides of a view and it centres. `layout_constraintHorizontal_bias` moves
it along that span: `0.0` sits at the start, `1.0` at the end, `0.5` is the centre and the
default.

```xml title="Bias.xml"
app:layout_constraintStart_toStartOf="parent"
app:layout_constraintEnd_toEndOf="parent"
app:layout_constraintHorizontal_bias="0.25"
```

This is how you place something a quarter of the way across without a guideline — and unlike a
fixed margin it holds on every screen width.
</Detail>

## Common mistakes

<Pitfall title="Four that produce a view in the corner">
**A missing constraint in one direction.** Constrain horizontally but not vertically and the view
sits at the top, silently. The editor flags it; the build does not.

**`match_parent` on a child.** Unsupported here. Use `0dp` with constraints on both sides.

**Nesting `ConstraintLayout` inside `ConstraintLayout`.** It works, and it throws away the reason
you chose it. Reach for a chain, a guideline or a barrier first.

**A guideline where a barrier belongs.** A fixed line looks right in English and breaks the
moment a translated label is longer.
</Pitfall>

## Practice

1. Place a view with only a horizontal constraint. Run it, then add the vertical one.
2. Build a three-view row as a chain, and cycle the head through `spread`, `spread_inside` and
   `packed`.
3. Make a two-column form with a guideline at 30%. Then replace the guideline with a barrier and
   double the length of one label.
4. Take a screen built from nested `LinearLayout`s and flatten it. Compare the depth in Layout
   Inspector.

## Recap

| Thing | One line |
| --- | --- |
| Constraint | *My side, to your side*; at least one per axis |
| `0dp` | Match constraints — the replacement for `match_parent` |
| `match_parent` | Not supported on a child |
| Chain | Mutually constrained views; the head carries the style |
| `spread` / `spread_inside` / `packed` | How a chain distributes space |
| Guideline | A fixed invisible line at a percent or dp |
| Barrier | A line that follows the largest referenced view |
| `bias` | Where along a constrained span the view sits |

<Interview title="A quick way to tell whether someone has built a real screen with it">
"When would you use a barrier rather than a guideline?"

When the line has to follow content rather than sit at a fixed place — a label column where the
longest label decides the width. Adding that this is what survives translation, because a
guideline tuned to English breaks when a label gets longer, is the part that sounds like it came
from a real screen rather than a tutorial.
</Interview>
